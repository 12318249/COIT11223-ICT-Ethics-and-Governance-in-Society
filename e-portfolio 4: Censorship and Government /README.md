Artefact 2: Nepal Social Media Ban

News article: Reuters – Nepal to block some social media including Facebook

<img width="452" height="207" alt="image" src="https://github.com/user-attachments/assets/cfaf65a1-adfb-44ee-a462-1cd835216010" />

 
Summary

In September 2025, Nepal's social media service providers were banned due to their failure to meet the registration process, according to Reuters. The government cited concerns on the issue of fake identity, hate speech, rumours, cybercrime and social stability as justification for the measures, but those who opposed them questioned freedom of expression (Sharma 2025).


Justification & Reflection

I chose this artefact because it shows the different interpretations of the same regulation by governments and citizens. Prior to studying Week 9, with censorship I would only think of things which are clearly illegal. I realised the Nepal situation was an example where access also plays a role in political communication / public participation. It confirmed that while assessing the safety goals set out is essential, it is equally important to pay attention to the broader repercussions of closing down communication platforms in democratic societies when thinking about responsible governance of ICTs.




Artefact 3: Social Media Moderation and Freedom of Expression

Journal article: Bousquet – Limiting the right to moderate: political equality, social media, and viewpoint-based moderation

<img width="452" height="221" alt="image" src="https://github.com/user-attachments/assets/2dbf0f5a-ef15-434f-ad07-f6ef952640fc" />

 
Summary

In the article "Viewpoints-based moderation by social-media platforms," Bousquet (2025, p. 1) looks at the role that major social-media platforms play in viewpoints-based moderation and identifies the potential harms of some forms of censorship, demotion and amplification that can undermine users' equal opportunity for political action. The article further sets out to differentiate these concerns from others that could be considered harmful speech worthy of regulation.


Justification & Reflection

I selected this piece due to its expansion of my understanding of censorship beyond government censorship. Since private platforms were not explicitly declared as fail, I hadn't thought of them as a way to run "private" services. As I read the article I thought how choice of platforms and how they are moderated could influence whose ideas are seen not to be ones for everyone. This will build straight on to discussing platforms, hosts, DNS providers and other intermediaries discussed in Week 9! I would thus look at social and technical mechanisms of moderation, together with the social results of such moderation, from an ICT perspective as a participant at the event as well as the role of public accountability in participation and equity.
